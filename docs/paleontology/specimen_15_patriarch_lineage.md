   - If per-base mutation rate is $\mu = 10^{-3}$ and genome length is $L = 60\text{ bits}$, the probability of an uncorrupted replication is $(1 - \mu)^L \approx 0.941$ per generation.

### 15.4 Formal Mathematics & Lineage Longevity <sup id="cite_ref-4" class="text-cyan-400 font-mono text-[10px]"><a href="#ref-4">[4]</a></sup> <sup id="cite_ref-3" class="text-cyan-400 font-mono text-[10px]"><a href="#ref-3">[3]</a></sup>
The probability of a lineage surviving uncorrupted for $T$ epochs under per-base mutation rate $\mu$ and genome length $L$ follows Poisson survival dynamics:

$$\textcolor{#38bdf8}{P_{\text{conservation}}(T)} = \textcolor{#34d399}{\left( (1 - \mu)^L \right)^T} \approx \textcolor{#fbbf24}{e^{-\mu L T}}$$

$$\textcolor{#38bdf8}{\bar{W}(t)} = \textcolor{#34d399}{W_0 \cdot (1 + a t)^b}$$

> [!DECODER]
> ### 🔍 Equation Decoder: Lineage Conservation & Lenski Fitness Trajectory
> - **$\textcolor{#38bdf8}{P_{\text{conservation}}(T)}$ (Conserved Genome Probability):** The mathematical probability that a 60-bit genome survives across $T$ generations without a single deleterious mutation.
> - **$\textcolor{#34d399}{\mu}$ (Per-Base Replication Mutation Rate):** Stochastic bit-flip probability during genome duplication in the `GenomeSafe` ($\mu \approx 10^{-3}$).
> - **$\textcolor{#34d399}{L}$ (Genome Sequence Length):** Total number of informational bits in the organism's hereditary tape ($L=60\text{ bits}$).
> - **$\textcolor{#fbbf24}{e^{-\mu L T}}$ (Exponential Mutational Sieve):** Proves that persistence for $T \gg \frac{1}{\mu L}$ requires intense purifying selection to actively purge corrupted variants.
> - **$\textcolor{#38bdf8}{\bar{W}(t)}$ (Mean Population Fitness):** Hyperbolic adaptation curve discovered by Richard Lenski in the 75,000-generation LTEE experiment, where fitness increases without bound following a power-law trajectory ($b \approx 0.1$).

### 15.6 Conceptual Misconception Immunity (Skeptic FAQ)
- **Q: If a lineage hasn't changed its DNA in 5,000 ticks, has it stopped evolving?**  
  *Population Genetics Answer:* No! This is the "Static Fossil Fallacy." Stabilizing (purifying) selection is an active, vigorous evolutionary process. Every generation, mutations arise; natural selection actively detects and eliminates deleterious mutants, defending the ancestral adaptation against mutational entropy. In nature, "living fossils" like the Coelacanth or the Horseshoe Crab are not evolutionary failures—they are evolutionary masterpieces whose adaptations were so optimal that purifying selection preserved them for hundreds of millions of years.
- **Q: Did the software developer freeze this lineage to preserve it?**  
  *Simulation Mechanics Answer:* No. Patriarch lineages run under the exact same physics engine, mutation rates, and thermal rules as all other cells. They survive solely because they colonized stable, sheltered basalt niches where their conserved metabolic netlist maintains positive energy balance without requiring risky genomic innovations.
- **Q: How does the Digital Paleontologist identify a Patriarch lineage?**  
  *Forensic Paleontology:* The `NaturalistAssistant` and `SimulationWorld` maintain an unalterable phylogenetic tree tracking founder IDs, generation counts, and chronological age. When a cell's lineage age exceeds $5,000\text{ ticks}$ with zero Hamming drift in its core netlist, it is classified as *Paterarchaeum lenskii*.
